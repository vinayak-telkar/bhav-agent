The problem

Price and volume alone can't tell a genuine buyer from a busy day.

What separates real accumulation from intraday churn — delivery percentage, and the footprints around it in the futures & options data — is buried in NSE's daily bhavcopy, a file built for clearing and settlement, not for reading. No retail investor opens it every evening. So they act late: buying into leveraged rallies, missing quiet accumulation, getting no warning when a large holder starts exiting.

An autonomous analyst that reads the bhavcopy every evening, checks only the stocks you actually hold or watch, and tells you — in plain English — what the flow says and what the sensible next step is. Product thesis

It is explicitly not an advisory product. Every read is data-driven information, framed the way a weather report is framed: here's what the instruments show, here's the confidence, you decide what to do about it. Every output resolves to one of seven defined actions — never a bare buy/sell call.

Hold, no action Review the position Stagger an entry / add on dip Defer entry Trim into strength Tighten exit discipline Add to watchlist
What makes it agentic

Three capabilities, not one prompt.

Agent Bhav isn't a single call to a language model. It's a small set of agents that each make their own decisions about what to look at, verify their own output before you see it, and remember what they told you last time.

The Daily DigestMode 1 · proactive

Runs through every stock you hold or watch, one at a time, and decides for itself how deep to go on each.

  • Pulls today's snapshot first, and only pulls the heavier data — 20-day delivery trend, F&O positioning — for stocks where something actually crossed a threshold. A quiet day gets a quiet read; the agent doesn't manufacture drama to justify itself.
  • Checks what it told you about that same stock last time before writing anything new, so a "hold" today either reinforces or explicitly revises yesterday's call — it doesn't restart from zero every evening.
  • Writes the read, then re-reads its own output: every number it just cited gets checked against the real data it pulled this turn. Anything it can't back up is discarded and replaced with a plain, honest, data-only summary — never a guess dressed up as an insight.
The Chat AnalystMode 2 · on demand

Ask it anything about your portfolio, whenever you want an answer.

  • It doesn't answer from memory or general market knowledge — it looks up your actual holdings, snapshot, or history through the same tools the digest uses, every single time it's asked.
  • Same verification discipline as the digest: a number it can't trace back to a real lookup this turn doesn't reach you.
  • Knows what it's for. Portfolio questions get answered in full; "should I buy this" or "write me code to automate my trading" get a polite decline — a boundary the agent checks and enforces itself, not a keyword filter bolted on after the fact.
The Self-Grading LoopInsight tracker · retrospective

The agent doesn't get to make a call and walk away from it.

  • Every insight is logged against the price at the time it was made.
  • Over the next several sessions, the agent checks back on its own, without being asked, to see whether the stock actually moved the way the signal implied.
  • Each call resolves to confirmed, expired, or strengthening — a track record you can see, not just an accumulating pile of opinions.
Why this is trustworthy

Every insight carries its own evidence, or it doesn't get shown to you.

Portfolio-relative, not market-wide. Every stock is measured against its own ~20-day baseline, and only threshold-crossers surface. "Nothing changed today" is a complete, valid, valuable answer — the agent isn't rewarded for finding something to say.

Evidence, not adjectives. "Delivery 68% vs 44% baseline over 8 sessions while price rose 6%" — never a bare "looks bullish." Ambiguous readings are labeled ambiguous, with the tie-breaker stated plainly.

Guardrails

An agent that can say "I don't know" is more useful than one that can't.

grounding

Verify before you see it

Every number in every narrative is checked against the real tool output from that turn. Fails verification, gets swapped for a plain data summary — silently, before it ever reaches the screen.

scope

A defined edge, held firmly

Portfolio questions, answered fully. Trade execution, automation, "should I buy" — declined, every time, by the agent's own judgment, not a keyword list.

privacy

Minimum data, by design

A symbol list and a size bucket. No name, no broker login, no portfolio value. There's nothing sensitive to leak because nothing sensitive is asked for.
